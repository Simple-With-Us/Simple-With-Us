CodeCaps Privacy Policy

Updated 19 October 2026.  CodeCaps is a centralized monitor for your AI subscription plans: it shows the quota, the reset window, and the pacing for the AI services you have already signed in to.  This policy covers the CodeCaps macOS menu bar app and the CodeCaps iPhone and iPad companion.

Overview

CodeCaps (we, our, or us) is a monitoring tool for AI subscription plans and the quota they include.  It reads usage data that already exists on your own devices and presents the quota remaining, the reset window, and the pacing in one place.

This policy covers two products:

  • The CodeCaps macOS menu bar app. The menu bar application that reads local AI CLI and session data on your Mac and shows quota readings in its menu and in a floating Picture in Picture window.
  • The CodeCaps iPhone and iPad companion (com.simplewithus.codecaps.ios).  The companion app and the home screen widget that ships with it, which show quota readings that arrive over the network from the endpoint you configure.

CodeCaps has no account, no sign-up, and no login of its own.  The quota readings stay on the devices you run CodeCaps on, and they only travel if you have chosen an endpoint to carry them.  The only data that reaches us without you asking for it is the diagnostic data described below.

The macOS Menu Bar App

Quota readings are computed from data that is already on your Mac.

  • It reads your local sign-ins. CodeCaps reads the local CLI and session data for the AI tools you have already signed in to — Claude, Codex, Cursor, Antigravity, xAI (Grok), MiniMax, and Muse — and uses it to work out the quota remaining and when the window resets.
  • You do not type provider API keys. Where a provider needs a credential, CodeCaps reuses the local sign-in your CLI already has instead of asking you for a key.
  • It reads local files only. Every reading comes from files and sessions on your Mac.  No CodeCaps server fetches anything on your behalf, because there is no CodeCaps server that does that.

Endpoint push and pull are off by default.  Nothing leaves your Mac until you turn them on in Settings.

When you enable them, you give the app an endpoint URL, and it pushes readings to that endpoint or pulls readings from it.  The endpoint is one you choose: a server you run, or a fleet sync service that you already have an account with.

A collector token, if you use one, is stored in the macOS Keychain.  It is never written to a plain text file, and it is never sent anywhere except to the endpoint you configured.

A separate, optional setup lets the owner of the Mac sync the app's own app-level settings — the two fleet endpoint URLs and a refresh interval — through Infisical, a secrets manager.  Your display preferences and alert settings never go there, and the read and push tokens stay in the macOS Keychain.

The iPhone and iPad Companion

The companion has no account, no login, and no subscription, and it collects no personal data about you.

It shows the quota readings for your AI subscription plans, and it gets them in one of two ways:

  • From an endpoint you configure. In Settings you enter an endpoint URL and a bearer token, and the companion pulls the readings from that endpoint over the network.  The readings are produced on the Mac, or wherever else you run CodeCaps, and they reach the phone through that endpoint: the macOS app pushes to it, and the companion pulls from it.
  • From the app group on this device. The companion and its home screen widget share one app group, so the widget reads the snapshot the companion has already fetched instead of making a request of its own.  That group is between the companion and its widget only: the macOS app is a separate product with its own group and never writes into it.

The bearer token is kept in the device's Keychain and is never stored in plain text.  It is sent only as the authorization header of a request to the endpoint you chose.

If you configure no endpoint, the companion shows an empty state and sends nothing anywhere, apart from the diagnostic data described below.

The home screen widget shows the same readings as the app.  It reads the snapshot the companion has already cached in their shared app group, and when you have configured an endpoint it refreshes from that same endpoint with the same bearer token and caches the result for the next refresh.  The widget collects no additional data and sends nothing anywhere except that endpoint.

Diagnostic Data

Both apps use Sentry, a crash reporting service, to collect technical diagnostic data so that we can find and fix crashes and performance problems.

  • Crash data. Crash reports from the app.
  • Performance data. App hangs longer than two seconds, a sample of traces, a sample of continuous profiles, and failed network requests.
  • Other diagnostic data. Device model, operating system version, app version, and an anonymous installation identifier.

This data is used for app functionality only, which means keeping the apps working and fixing problems in them.  It is not linked to your identity, it is not used for tracking, and it is not used for advertising or marketing.

The specifics:

  • The diagnostic SDK is configured to send no personal information by default, and no screenshot and no view hierarchy is attached to a crash report.
  • Query parameters named token, key, secret, auth, or password are redacted on the device before an event is sent.  Read tokens, quota payloads, and provider credentials are never attached to an event.
  • On the iPhone and iPad, Sentry can record a short replay of the app's own screens to help diagnose a problem.  Text and images are masked before the recording leaves the device, so the shape of the screen is captured and the values on it are not.  The macOS app does not record screen replays.
  • The companion's privacy manifest declares tracking as off and declares no tracking domains.

What We Do Not Do

  • No advertising. We do not show advertising in CodeCaps, and we do not use advertising networks.
  • No selling or renting data. We do not sell, rent, trade, or monetize your readings, your diagnostic data, or anything else.
  • No cross-app tracking. We do not track you across other apps or websites, and the companion reads no advertising identifier.
  • No third-party analytics. Neither app links an advertising or product-analytics SDK.  The macOS app links Sentry for crash reporting and Sparkle for signed in-app updates, and the companion links Sentry for crash reporting; neither is used for advertising or marketing.

Your Choices and Deletion

  • Turn endpoint push or pull off in the macOS app's Settings at any time, and the app stops sending readings.
  • Clear the sync token in the companion's Settings to stop the app from contacting your endpoint.
  • Delete the app to remove the readings and the token it stored on the device.

If you would like diagnostic data removed, email us and we will ask Sentry to delete it.

Children

CodeCaps is a productivity tool and is not directed at children.  The companion collects no personal data about anyone, including children.

Changes to This Policy

If this policy changes, we will update the date at the top of the page and keep the new version at the same address.

Contact

Questions about this policy, about the data CodeCaps handles, or a deletion request are all welcome.  Email feedback@simplewithus.com or contact@simplewithus.com, or use the CodeCaps support page.

Please do not email passwords, API keys, bearer tokens, or unredacted personal data.
